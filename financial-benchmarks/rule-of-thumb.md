# Credit Ratio Rule-of-Thumb Thresholds

Thresholds applied to ratios where Damodaran (or other public sources) do not publish industry medians. These are widely-cited lending-convention thresholds, not rating-tier benchmarks. They mirror what a third-party lender would treat as broadly acceptable.

| Ratio | Investment-grade rule-of-thumb threshold | Sub-IG signal |
|---|---|---|
| Asset Coverage (Total assets / Debt) | > 1.0x | < 1.0x |

## Source notes

**Asset Coverage** — basic going-concern test for unsecured creditors: total tangible assets that could be liquidated in a wind-down exceed the debt obligation. The 1.0x threshold reflects long-standing lending convention; values well above 1.0x (typically 2.5x+) suggest meaningful asset backing even for unsecured lending. Below 1.0x indicates the borrower could not satisfy its debt obligation from asset liquidation alone, signalling balance-sheet stress regardless of cash flow.

This is a qualitative complement to the cash-flow-based ratios sourced from Damodaran; it does not substitute for them.

## When to apply

The rule-of-thumb threshold is the **fallback verdict driver** when industry-median data is unavailable. Where Damodaran provides an industry median for the ratio in question, prefer the median-comparison verdict over the rule-of-thumb threshold.

The Financial Capacity Analysis section uses three verdict types:

1. **Peer-median comparison** — borrower's ratio compared to Damodaran industry median (preferred where available).
2. **Rule-of-thumb threshold** — for ratios with no public industry median (Asset Coverage).
3. **Loan-specific serviceability check** — for the cash-against-loan calculation (separate section, not in the ratio table).
