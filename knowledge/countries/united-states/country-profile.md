## 1. Tax Authority and Legal Framework

### Quick Reference
- Tax authority: Internal Revenue Service (IRS) (EY 2025)
- Core statutory provision: IRC § 482 (OECD, EY 2025)
- Core regulations: Treas. Reg. §§ 1.482-1 through 1.482-9 (pricing rules); Treas. Reg. §§ 1.6662-1 through 1.6662-6 (penalties) (EY 2025)
- Key revenue procedures: Rev. Procs. 99-32, 2015-40, 2015-41, 2007-13, 2005-46 (EY 2025)
- Corporate tax rate: 21% (TCJA, effective 1 January 2018) (EY 2025)

### Detailed Regulatory Framework

The US TP framework is codified in Treas. Reg. sections 1.482-1 through 1.482-9. The OECD TPG are not formally incorporated into US law, but all US regulations are consistent with the TPG. The TPG are not given binding effect or subsidiary application status. (OECD)

The US is an OECD member jurisdiction. The IRS considers its transfer pricing laws and regulations to be wholly consistent with OECD Guidelines. For domestic purposes, OECD Guidelines may provide support in certain instances when addressed by the IRS (e.g., APA and MAP) but would not be directly relevant to the application of any US transfer pricing methods. If taxpayers pursue competent authority relief from double taxation or a bilateral APA, the OECD Guidelines are relevant and may be used to demonstrate compliance with international principles. (EY 2025)

Notice 2025-4 cross-references the Amount B Report as the sole explicit TPG reference. (OECD)

#### Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) — Transfer Pricing Implications

The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017, effective from 1 January 2018, made significant changes to the IRC, including lowering the US corporate income tax rate to 21% and introducing a number of new provisions with transfer pricing implications (EY 2025):

- **Base Erosion and Anti-Abuse Tax (BEAT):** The BEAT minimum tax detailed in IRC § 59A is the excess (if any) of 10% (increasing to 12.5% for tax years beginning in calendar year 2026) of an applicable taxpayer's "modified taxable income" over an adjusted regular tax liability amount for the tax year. (EY 2025)

- **Foreign Derived Intangible Income (FDII):** IRC § 250 provides a special deduction for a US corporation's FDII. This new section provides a lower rate of tax on a portion of profits in connection with (i) property sold to any non-US person for foreign use or (ii) services provided to a person, or with respect to property, not located within the US. IRC § 250 generally allows a domestic corporation an annual deduction for its Global Intangible Low-Taxed Income (GILTI) inclusion under IRC § 951A and FDII. For tax years beginning after 31 December 2017, but on or before 31 December 2025, the IRC § 250 deduction generally is the sum of (i) 50% of the corporation's GILTI inclusion amount (and IRC § 78 "gross-up" for associated deemed-paid foreign income taxes) and (ii) 37.5% of its FDII. If the sum of the taxpayer's GILTI and FDII amounts exceed the taxpayer's taxable income, however, the IRC § 250 deduction is reduced proportionately to those two amounts. (EY 2025)

- **GILTI:** The GILTI regime is detailed in IRC §§ 250 and 951A and in revised IRC §§ 960 and 904. Generally, under IRC § 951A, a corporation can deduct 50% of its GILTI and claim a foreign tax credit (FTC) for 80% of foreign taxes paid or accrued on GILTI. (EY 2025)

- The TCJA moved the definition of intangibles from IRC § 936 to IRC § 367(d)(4) and expanded the definition. IRC § 367(d)(4) inter alia includes goodwill, going concern value and workforce in place. (EY 2025)

#### Transfer Pricing Examination Process

Key guidance includes: (1) Internal Revenue Manual § 4.61.3, Development of IRC 482 Cases; (2) Publication 5300 (2020), providing a guide to best practices and processes to assist with the planning, execution and resolution of transfer pricing examinations (must be shared with taxpayers at the start of an examination, intended to be consistent with the Large Business & International (LB&I) Examination Process (LEP) (Publication 5125)); (3) FAQs on TP Documentation (14 April 2020); and (4) Interim Guidance Memorandum on Economic Substance Doctrine and Related Penalties (22 April 2022) LB&I-04-0422-0014. (EY 2025)

## 2. Arm's Length Principle and Related Parties

### Quick Reference
- Arm's length standard applies via IRC § 482 and Treas. Reg. §§ 1.482-1 through 1.482-9 (OECD)
- Related parties = "controlled taxpayers" — facts-and-circumstances test, no fixed ownership threshold (OECD)
- Domestic transactions within consolidated return: not subject to TP obligations (EY 2025)
- Local branch of a foreign company must comply with US TP rules (EY 2025)

### Detailed Rules

**Related parties:** Defined as "controlled taxpayers" — two or more taxpayers owned or controlled directly or indirectly by the same interests. This is a facts-and-circumstances test with no pre-determined ownership thresholds. Control includes any kind of control, direct or indirect, whether legally enforceable or not. (Treas. Reg. sections 1.482-1(i)(4) and (5)) (OECD)

There is no US federal tax documentation obligation for domestic transactions between related parties who are part of the same consolidated US federal tax return. However, the Securities Exchange Commission requires publicly traded US companies to disclose material transactions with their subsidiaries on their annual financial statements and filings. (EY 2025)

A local branch of a foreign company must comply with the local transfer pricing rules. (EY 2025)

## 3. Transfer Pricing Methods

### Quick Reference
- Best method rule — no hierarchy of methods (OECD, EY 2025)
- Tangible goods: CUP, resale price, cost plus, CPM, profit split, unspecified methods (EY 2025)
- Intangibles: CUT, CPM, profit split, unspecified methods (EY 2025)
- Services: services cost, comparable uncontrolled services price, gross services margin, cost of services plus, CPM, profit split, unspecified methods (EY 2025)
- CSA buy-ins: CUT, income, acquisition price, market capitalization, residual profit split, unspecified methods (EY 2025)

### Detailed Rules

**Available methods:** CUP, Resale Price, Cost Plus, TNMM, Profit Split, and others including income, market cap, and acquisition price methods. (Treas. Reg. sections 1.482-3(a), -4(a), -7(g)(1), -9(a)) (OECD)

**Selection criterion:** The "best method" rule applies — there is no hierarchy of methods. The method that provides the most reliable measure of arm's length result must be used. (Treas. Reg. section 1.482-1(c)) (OECD)

The regulations provide a best-method rule for determining the appropriate method to be applied by the taxpayer for each intercompany transaction. (EY 2025)

**Commodity transactions:** No specific guidance, but Treas. Reg. 1.482-3(b)(5) is consistent with TPG paragraphs 2.18-2.22. (OECD)

## 4. Comparability Analysis and Benchmarking

### Quick Reference
- No preference for domestic over foreign comparables (OECD, EY 2025)
- Secret comparables not permitted (OECD)
- Interquartile range acceptable (OECD, EY 2025)
- If IRS adjusts using interquartile range, adjustment is generally to the median (EY 2025)
- Multi-year data acceptable depending on method and circumstances (EY 2025)
- Fresh benchmarking search: roll-forward acceptable for 2-3 years if fact pattern unchanged (EY 2025)
- Individual testing of transactions preferred (EY 2025)

### Detailed Rules

Consistent with TPG Chapter III. Key rules (OECD):

- **No preference** for domestic over foreign comparables. (Treas. Reg. sections 1.482-1(c) and (d)) (OECD)
- **Secret comparables** not permitted for TP assessment purposes. (OECD)
- **Statistical measures:** Interquartile ranges and other statistical methods may be used to improve reliability. (Treas. Reg. sections 1.482-1(e) and 1.482-7(g)(2)(ix)) (OECD)
- **Comparability adjustments** required where they improve reliability with respect to material differences. (Treas. Reg. section 1.482-1(d)(2)) (OECD)

**Interquartile range definition:** The interquartile range is the range from the 25th to the 75th percentile of the results derived from the uncontrolled comparables. For this purpose, the 25th percentile is the lowest result derived from an uncontrolled comparable such that at least 25 percent of the results are at or below the value of that result. However, if exactly 25 percent of the results are at or below a result, then the 25th percentile is equal to the average of that result and the next higher result derived from the uncontrolled comparables. The 75th percentile is determined analogously. (EY 2025)

**Adjustment point:** If the interquartile range is used, the regulations state that an adjustment will generally be to the median of all results. (EY 2025)

**Multi-year analysis:** The results of a controlled transaction ordinarily will be compared with the results of uncontrolled comparables occurring in the taxable year under review. It may be appropriate, however, to consider data related to the uncontrolled comparables or the controlled taxpayer for one or more years before or after the year under review. If data related to uncontrolled comparables from multiple years is used, data related to the controlled taxpayer for the same years ordinarily must be considered. The extent to which it is appropriate to consider multiple years' data depends on the method being applied and the transactions addressed. Circumstances that may warrant the consideration of data from multiple years include the extent to which complete and accurate data is available for the taxable year under review, the effect of business cycles in the controlled taxpayer's industry, and the effects of life cycles of the product or intangible property being examined. (EY 2025)

**Benchmarking refresh:** There is no legal requirement for a fresh benchmarking search every year, as roll-forward and financial updates are acceptable for up to two to three years (if the fact pattern has remained the same). (EY 2025)

**Benchmarking results type:** No stipulated requirement; the choice of simple, weighted, or pooled results will depend on the facts and circumstances and comparability. (EY 2025)

**Aggregation vs. individual testing:** Currently, there are no specific guidelines regarding aggregate testing of transactions. Accordingly, testing of individual transactions is the preferred approach. Treas. Reg. § 1.482-1T(a)(i)(B) states that "the combined effect of two or more separate transactions ... may be considered if the transactions, taken as a whole, are so interrelated that an aggregate analysis ... provides the most reliable measure of an arm's length result." However, this temporary regulation expired in September 2018 and was not replaced. (EY 2025)

- CSA transfers of existing intangibles: Treas. Reg. § 1.482-7(g) (OECD)
- Tangible goods with embedded intangibles: Treas. Reg. § 1.482-3(f) (OECD)

## 6. Hard-to-Value Intangibles and Commensurate with Income

### Quick Reference
- US equivalent of OECD HTVI = "commensurate with income" (CWI) principle (OECD)
- CWI is broader than HTVI — applies to ALL intercompany intangible property transfers (OECD)
- Periodic adjustments available; exceptions exist for highly comparable CUTs and unanticipated results (OECD)
- CWI effectively overrides 3-year statute of limitations; exception waiting periods of 5 or 10 years (OECD)
- APAs available for HTVI-scope transactions (OECD)

### Detailed Rules

The US equivalent of the OECD HTVI concept is the "commensurate with income" (CWI) principle with periodic adjustment rules. The CWI standard is broader than HTVI — it applies to ALL intercompany intangible property transfers, not just hard-to-value ones. (IRC section 482, second sentence; Treas. Reg. sections 1.482-4(f)(2) and (6), 1.482-7(i)(6)) (OECD)

Key features (OECD):

- **Scope:** Any intercompany transfer of intangible property.
- **Periodic adjustments:** Must be consistent with the arm's length principle. Separate rules for IP transfers (section 1.482-4(f)) and cost contribution agreements (section 1.482-7(i)(6)).
- **Exceptions:** Adjustments cannot be made if ex ante pricing is arm's length by reference to a highly comparable uncontrolled transaction, or when results were not reasonably anticipated.
- **Statute of limitations:** The CWI principle effectively overrides the standard 3-year statute. Exceptions require waiting periods of 5 or even 10 years, indicating adjustments can be made beyond that period.
- **Hindsight protections:** Multiple provisions ensure periodic adjustments are not an inappropriate use of hindsight.
- **APAs available:** Bilateral and multilateral APAs can be requested for HTVI-scope transactions.
- **Multiple adjustments:** The IRS can make several adjustments for a single HTVI transaction.
- **Open/closed year adjustments:** Possible to adjust in open years for amounts pertaining to closed years, including corresponding adjustments (generally via MAP request).

## 8. Financial Transactions

### Quick Reference
- Intercompany loans: Treas. Reg. § 1.482-2 (OECD)
- Safe haven interest rate: 100%-130% of Applicable Federal Rate (AFR) for certain intercompany loans (OECD, EY 2025)
- No other safe harbors per se (EY 2025)
- Interest deduction limitation: IRC § 163(j) (OECD, EY 2025)
- Hybrid arrangements: IRC § 267A (OECD)

### Detailed Rules

Treas. Reg. section 1.482-2 covers intercompany loans, consistent with TPG Chapter X. The US framework does not cover all financial transaction types addressed in Chapter X. (OECD)

**Loan safe haven interest rate:** 100% to 130% of the Applicable Federal Rate for certain intercompany loans. (Treas. Reg. section 1.482-2(a)(2)(iii)) (OECD) There are no other safe harbors per se. (EY 2025)

**Outside the TP framework (OECD):**
- IRC section 163(j): limits interest deductions (BEPS Action 4). Earnings stripping rules under IRC § 163(j) are intended to prevent the erosion of the US tax base of a thinly capitalized corporation by means of excessive deductions for certain interest. (EY 2025)
- IRC section 267A: limits deductions for interest and payments in hybrid situations (BEPS Action 2)

## 10. Transfer Pricing Documentation

### Quick Reference
- Documentation is **voluntary** but provides critical penalty protection (OECD, EY 2025, KPMG 2025)
- No Master File requirement (all sources)
- No Local File requirement (all sources); however, OECD MF + LF format "substantially complies" with US TP documentation standards (Deloitte 2021)
- BEPS Action 13 format documentation is NOT sufficient to achieve penalty protection (EY 2025)
- No materiality threshold for TP documentation (EY 2025, KPMG 2025)
- Must be prepared by tax return filing date for penalty protection (EY 2025, KPMG 2025, Deloitte 2021)
- Must be provided to IRS within 30 days of examiner's request (EY 2025, KPMG 2025)
- Not required to be disclosed with or attached to return (EY 2025)
- Stand-alone reports per entity not required for MNEs with multiple US entities (EY 2025)
- Annual preparation required; changes from prior year must be reflected (EY 2025)
- Language: English (EY 2025, KPMG 2025)

### Detailed Rules — Penalty Protection Documentation

Although transfer pricing documentation is not required by domestic law, in practice it is recommended that taxpayers maintain contemporaneous documentation as a defense against transfer pricing penalties. The transfer pricing documentation must be in existence when the return is filed to obtain penalty protection. But the existence of documentation need not be either disclosed in, or provided with, the domestic return. Taxpayers must provide the documentation to the IRS within 30 days of an examiner's request to receive protection from penalties. (EY 2025)

In order to obtain penalty protection, transfer pricing documentation needs to be complete by the tax return submission date. Taxpayers should maintain records supporting preparation date including report date, file save date, transmittal letter, etc. (KPMG 2025)

For penalty avoidance purposes, a taxpayer satisfies the documentation requirement if it maintains certain documentation that substantiates the taxpayer's assertion that its choice and application of a method were reasonable and that the method provided the most reliable measure of an arm's-length result under the best-method rule. (EY 2025)

The principal documents required by the regulations are (Treas. Reg. section 1.6662-6(d)) (EY 2025, OECD):

1. An overview of the taxpayer's business and an analysis of the legal and economic factors that affect the pricing of its property or services
2. A description of the organizational structure (including an organization chart) covering all related parties engaged in transactions potentially relevant under IRC § 482, including foreign affiliates whose transactions directly or indirectly affect the pricing of property or services in the United States
3. Any documents explicitly required by regulations under IRC § 482 (e.g., cost-sharing arrangement documents)
4. A description of the pricing method selected and reasons the method was selected (i.e., a best-method analysis)
5. A description of alternative methods that were considered and why they were not selected
6. A description of controlled transactions and any internal data used to analyze those transactions
7. A description of the comparables used, how comparability was evaluated, and any adjustments that were made
8. An explanation of any economic analysis and any projections relied upon to develop the pricing method
9. Any material data discovered after the close of the tax year but before the filing of the tax return, which would help to determine if a taxpayer selected and applied a method in a reasonable manner
10. A general index of the principal and background documents and a description of the record-keeping system used for cataloging and accessing those documents

The Local File documentation for the US should be consistent with the regulations under IRC §§ 482 and 6662. (EY 2025)

### Country-by-Country Reporting

- **Threshold:** USD 850 million of annual global revenue (approximately EUR 700 million) (EY 2025, KPMG 2025, OECD)
- **Form:** Form 8975 and Schedule A (Form 8975) (EY 2025)
- **Filing:** US MNEs filing Form 8975 and Schedule A should file a separate Schedule A for each tax jurisdiction where the MNE group operates and list all constituent entities resident in the tax jurisdiction. A US MNE group with only fiscally transparent US business entities would not provide a Schedule A for the US but would provide a Schedule A for "stateless" entities. (EY 2025)
- **Notification:** No CbCR notification requirement in the US (EY 2025, KPMG 2025, Deloitte 2021)
- **Filing deadline:** With the tax return for the respective year (EY 2025, Deloitte 2021)
- **First effective year:** Starting 1 July 2016 (Deloitte 2021); effective commencement date 30 June 2017 (EY 2025)
- **Secondary filing requirement:** No (Deloitte 2021)
- **Parent Surrogate Filing Available:** Yes (Deloitte 2021)
- **MCAA signatory:** No. The US is not a signatory of the Multilateral Competent Authority Agreement on the exchange of CbCR. (EY 2025, KPMG 2025)
- **Filing scope:** Regulations only imposed on MNEs headquartered in the jurisdiction, or designated surrogate parent entities located in the jurisdiction (KPMG 2025)
- **Material differences from OECD format:** No material differences from OECD format (EY 2025)
- **Public CbCR:** Not applicable — no public CbCR legislation in the US (EY 2025)

### Other Required Filings

- **Form 5472:** Information returns for controlled transactions (OECD, EY 2025)
- **Forms 5471, 5472, 8865:** Required regarding transactions with related parties (EY 2025)
- **Schedule UTP (Form 1120):** Certain taxpayers must disclose uncertain tax positions. Required for corporations filing Forms 1120, 1120-F, 1120-L, or 1120-PC with assets of USD 10 million or more for the tax year and the corporation recorded a liability for unrecognized tax benefits for a tax position in audited financial statements. (EY 2025)

## 11. Deadlines

### Quick Reference
- Corporate tax return: 15th day of the 4th month after year-end (typically 15 April); 6-month extension available (EY 2025)
- TP documentation preparation: by tax return filing date (EY 2025, KPMG 2025, Deloitte 2021)
- TP documentation preparation with extension: prepare by 15th April, typically extended until 15th October (Deloitte 2021)
- Submission to IRS: within 30 days of examiner's request (EY 2025, KPMG 2025)
- CbCR filing: with tax return for the respective year (EY 2025, Deloitte 2021)

### Detailed Rules

The deadline for the corporate tax return is the 15th day of the fourth month following the end of the corporation's tax year. A six-month extension generally is available. (EY 2025)

If the documentation is prepared to help protect against penalties, then it must be in existence by the filing date of a US tax return that has been filed in a timely manner. (EY 2025)

There is no statutory deadline for submission of transfer pricing documentation or Local File. The submission is based on the request of tax authorities. Taxpayers must provide the documentation to the IRS within 30 days of an examiner's request. (EY 2025, KPMG 2025)

## 12. Penalties

### Quick Reference
- No penalty for failure to maintain/provide TP documentation per se (EY 2025, KPMG 2025)
- Documentation protects against valuation misstatement penalties (all sources)
- 20% penalty: substantial valuation misstatement (EY 2025, KPMG 2025)
- 40% penalty: gross valuation misstatement (EY 2025, KPMG 2025)
- Interest charged on underpayments using AFRs (EY 2025)
- Penalty relief available via reasonable cause and good faith (EY 2025)
- No criminal prosecution risk for TP matters (EY 2025)

### Detailed Rules

Pursuant to IRC § 6662, taxpayers may be liable for either a 20% or 40% penalty for an underpayment of tax attributable to a substantial or gross valuation misstatement. (EY 2025)

There are no penalties directly associated with failure to submit transfer pricing documentation, but documentation can protect from penalties that would otherwise be levied in the event of a transfer pricing adjustment by the tax authority. Regulations supply detailed requirements that the documentation must satisfy. (KPMG 2025)

#### Transactional Penalty (KPMG 2025)
- **20% rate:** Applies where the misstated transfer price for any property or service is 200 percent or more, or 50 percent or less, of the correct price.
- **40% rate:** Applies if the misstated transfer price is 400 percent or more, or 25 percent or less, of the correct price.

#### Net Adjustment Penalty (KPMG 2025)
- **20% rate:** Applies if the total net transfer pricing adjustment for the year is more than USD 5 million or 10 percent of gross receipts.
- **40% rate:** Applies if the adjustment is more than USD 20 million or 20 percent of gross receipts.

**Interest:** Interest is charged on underpayments using Applicable Federal Rates (AFRs). (EY 2025)

**Penalty relief:** Penalties may be avoided by establishing reasonable cause and good faith through taxpayer-provided documentation demonstrating the taxpayer's application of IRC § 482. (EY 2025)

**Incomplete or non-contemporaneous documentation:** There is no penalty for incomplete or non-contemporaneous documentation, but such documentation may not provide penalty protection. (EY 2025)

**Criminal prosecution:** No risk of criminal prosecution for transfer pricing matters. (EY 2025)

## 13. Statute of Limitations

### Quick Reference
- General: 3 years from the later of the return due date or the date the return was actually filed (EY 2025)
- Substantial understatement: 6 years (EY 2025)
- Fraud: no statute of limitations (EY 2025)
- CWI/periodic adjustments: effectively override the 3-year statute with 5- or 10-year waiting periods (OECD)
- MAP access to closed years: most treaties permit IRS access to closed years for double taxation relief (EY 2025)

### Detailed Rules

A general statute of limitations applies in the US — three years from the later of either the tax return due date or the date the return was actually filed. The statute is extended to six years for substantial understatements of income. There is no statute of limitations for fraud-related adjustments. Most treaties with trading partners provide the IRS access to closed years in order to provide relief from double taxation pursuant to a MAP. (EY 2025)

Under the commensurate with income principle, the CWI principle effectively overrides the standard 3-year statute. Exceptions require waiting periods of 5 or even 10 years, indicating adjustments can be made beyond that period. It is possible to adjust in open years for amounts pertaining to closed years, including corresponding adjustments (generally via MAP request). (OECD)

## 14. Transfer Pricing Audit Environment

### Quick Reference
- TP scrutiny during audit is common practice (EY 2025)
- IRS has recently increased focus on international transactions with emphasis on TP (EY 2025)
- High-risk areas: cost sharing, IP migration, BEPS-type transactions (EY 2025)
- Adjustment upon challenge is likely once IRS commits resources (EY 2025)
- Adjustment point: generally to the median when interquartile range is used (EY 2025)
- No downward self-help: taxpayer cannot file amended return to decrease taxable income (OECD)

### Detailed Rules

Transfer pricing is extensively regulated in the US, and the IRS has recently taken a number of administrative steps to increase its ability to focus on international transactions, with a particular emphasis on transfer pricing. Due to this emphasis, documentation is requested frequently at the outset of any examination of taxpayers transacting with foreign related parties. (EY 2025)

If the transfer pricing methodology for international transactions is challenged during the initial stages of any audit an adjustment is likely. Once the IRS commits significant resources to the audit, a Notice of Proposed Adjustment should be expected. However, experiences have shown that well-reasoned documentation may potentially reduce the possibility of further scrutiny. (EY 2025)

Cost sharing and other IP migration transactions are generally challenged. Other high-risk transactions, such as those described in the OECD BEPS Action Plan, also draw scrutiny. (EY 2025)

**No downward self-help:** A controlled taxpayer cannot file an amended return to decrease taxable income. Only the IRS can make adjustments. (Treas. Reg. section 1.482-1(a)(3)) (OECD)

**Year-end adjustments:** Allowed, subject to Treas. Reg. section 1.482-1(a)(3). (OECD)

**Secondary adjustments:** Allowed — may be treated as a dividend, capital contribution (Treas. Reg. section 1.482-1(g)(3)), or electively as a loan (Rev. Proc. 99-32). (OECD)

## 15. Advance Pricing Agreements (APAs)

### Quick Reference
- Types: unilateral, bilateral, multilateral (EY 2025, OECD)
- Administered by: IRS Advance Pricing and Mutual Agreement Program (APMA) (EY 2025)
- Guidance: Rev. Proc. 2015-41 (APAs); Rev. Proc. 2015-40 (competent authority) (EY 2025, OECD)
- Preferred term: 5-year prospective (EY 2025)
- Roll-backs: available (EY 2025)
- User fee charged (EY 2025)

### Detailed Rules

Taxpayers may request unilateral, bilateral or multilateral APAs. The APA process is administered by the IRS Advance Pricing and Mutual Agreement Program. Guidance regarding APAs can be found in Rev. Proc. 2015-41. The revenue procedure has strict case management procedures, disclosure requirements, and detailed guidance regarding the submission and processing of APA requests. Additional competent authority guidance is provided in Rev. Proc. 2015-40. In April of 2023, the IRS released an internal memorandum with guidance on review and acceptance of APA submission. The IRS charges a user fee for APAs. (EY 2025)

The IRS prefers an APA request to cover a prospective five-year term. (EY 2025)

Roll-backs are applicable. Because most US APAs have a prospective five-year term, the addition of a roll-back term could allow a taxpayer to resolve transfer pricing issues from older years in a single negotiation process. (EY 2025)

## 16. Mutual Agreement Procedure (MAP)

### Quick Reference
- Available under Rev. Proc. 2015-40 (EY 2025)
- Requires taxation not in accordance with applicable DTT (EY 2025)
- Taxpayer must be resident in the US or other contracting state (EY 2025)
- Time limit: prescribed period from first notification; varies by treaty (EY 2025)
- US-initiated adjustment: must be communicated in writing (e.g., Form 5701) before taxpayer may seek competent authority assistance (EY 2025)
- Other mechanisms: enhanced engagement/cooperative compliance programmes, ICAP (OECD)

### Detailed Rules

According to Rev. Proc. 2015-40, taxpayers may request MAP assistance often referred to as a "competent authority request" or a "MAP request," if taxation has or is likely to occur that is not in accordance with the provisions of a double tax treaty (DTT) to which the US is signatory. In addition, the taxpayer must be a resident either in the US or in the other relevant contracting state, meet the prescribed time limits, and satisfy the prescribed conditions for a competent authority request. Most of the US DTTs permit taxpayers to present a case to the IRS within a prescribed period from the first notification to the taxpayer of the actions giving rise to taxation not in accordance with the DTT. However, time limits may vary, and the relevant DTT should be consulted for the applicable time limit. For a US-initiated adjustment, generally the adjustment must be communicated in writing to the taxpayer (e.g., Form 5701, Notice of Proposed Adjustment) before the taxpayer may seek competent authority assistance. (EY 2025)

Other dispute resolution mechanisms available include enhanced engagement / cooperative compliance programmes and the International Compliance Assurance Programme (ICAP). (OECD)

## 17. Simplified and Streamlined Approach (Amount B)

### Quick Reference
- Adopted via Notice 2025-4 (released 18 December 2024) (OECD)
- Elective safe harbour for in-scope tested parties resident in the US (OECD)
- Effective: taxable years beginning on or after 1 January 2025 (OECD)
- OES upper bound: generally 30%, can be lower (not less than 20%) per Notice 2025-4, section 3.02(2) (OECD)
- US respects SSA outcomes applied by both covered and non-covered jurisdictions (OECD)

### Detailed Rules

The US adopted the SSA via Notice 2025-4 (released 18 December 2024). Key features (OECD):

- **Elective** for in-scope tested parties resident in the US (safe harbour, not a mandatory rule).
- **Effective:** Taxable years beginning on or after 1 January 2025.
- **OES upper bound:** Generally 30%, but can be lower (not less than 20%) per Notice 2025-4, section 3.02(2).
- **Respects outcomes:** The US respects SSA outcomes applied by both covered and non-covered jurisdictions.

## 18. PE Profit Attribution

### Quick Reference
- 57 US treaties: profits attributed based on the business profits article (OECD)
- 7 post-2010 treaties apply the Authorised OECD Approach (AOA): Belgium, Bulgaria, Canada, Germany, Iceland, Japan, United Kingdom (OECD)
- AOA not applied to pre-2010 treaties (OECD)
- US Model tax treaty follows AOA as preferred position for new treaties (OECD)

### Detailed Rules

For 57 US treaties, profits are attributed based on the business profits article. Seven treaties (post-2010) apply the Authorised OECD Approach (AOA): Belgium, Bulgaria, Canada, Germany, Iceland, Japan, United Kingdom. The US does not apply the AOA to pre-2010 treaties. The US Model tax treaty follows the AOA as the preferred position for new treaties. (OECD)
