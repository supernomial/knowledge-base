# United States Transfer Pricing Profile

## Arm's Length Principle

The US TP framework is codified in Treas. Reg. sections 1.482-1 through 1.482-9. The OECD TPG are not formally incorporated into US law, but all US regulations are consistent with the TPG. The TPG are not given binding effect or subsidiary application status. (Notice 2025-4 cross-references the Amount B Report as the sole explicit TPG reference.)

**Related parties:** Defined as "controlled taxpayers" — two or more taxpayers owned or controlled directly or indirectly by the same interests. This is a facts-and-circumstances test with no pre-determined ownership thresholds. Control includes any kind of control, direct or indirect, whether legally enforceable or not. (Treas. Reg. sections 1.482-1(i)(4) and (5))

## Transfer Pricing Methods

**Available methods:** CUP, Resale Price, Cost Plus, TNMM, Profit Split, and others including income, market cap, and acquisition price methods. (Treas. Reg. sections 1.482-3(a), -4(a), -7(g)(1), -9(a))

**Selection criterion:** The "best method" rule applies — there is no hierarchy of methods. The method that provides the most reliable measure of arm's length result must be used. (Treas. Reg. section 1.482-1(c))

**Commodity transactions:** No specific guidance, but Treas. Reg. 1.482-3(b)(5) is consistent with TPG paragraphs 2.18-2.22.

## Comparability Analysis

Consistent with TPG Chapter III. Key rules:

- **No preference** for domestic over foreign comparables. (Treas. Reg. sections 1.482-1(c) and (d))
- **Secret comparables** not permitted for TP assessment purposes.
- **Statistical measures:** Interquartile ranges and other statistical methods may be used to improve reliability. (Treas. Reg. sections 1.482-1(e) and 1.482-7(g)(2)(ix))
- **Comparability adjustments** required where they improve reliability with respect to material differences. (Treas. Reg. section 1.482-1(d)(2))

## Intangible Property

Treas. Reg. section 1.482-4 provides general guidance consistent with TPG Chapter VI. Additional rules:

- Section 1.482-7(g): transfers of existing intangibles in cost contribution arrangements (consistent with Chapters VI and VIII)
- Section 1.482-3(f): coordination rule for tangible property with embedded intangibles
- Section 1.482-9(m)(2): services transactions that effect a transfer of IP

**Outside the TP framework:** IRC section 367(d) requires income inclusions for certain outbound transfers of IP in tax-free reorganizations.

## Hard-to-Value Intangibles

The US equivalent of the OECD HTVI concept is the "commensurate with income" (CWI) principle with periodic adjustment rules. The CWI standard is broader than HTVI — it applies to ALL intercompany intangible property transfers, not just hard-to-value ones. (IRC section 482, second sentence; Treas. Reg. sections 1.482-4(f)(2) and (6), 1.482-7(i)(6))

Key features:

- **Scope:** Any intercompany transfer of intangible property.
- **Periodic adjustments:** Must be consistent with the arm's length principle. Separate rules for IP transfers (section 1.482-4(f)) and cost contribution agreements (section 1.482-7(i)(6)).
- **Exceptions:** Adjustments cannot be made if ex ante pricing is arm's length by reference to a highly comparable uncontrolled transaction, or when results were not reasonably anticipated.
- **Statute of limitations:** The CWI principle effectively overrides the standard 3-year statute. Exceptions require waiting periods of 5 or even 10 years, indicating adjustments can be made beyond that period.
- **Hindsight protections:** Multiple provisions ensure periodic adjustments are not an inappropriate use of hindsight.
- **APAs available:** Bilateral and multilateral APAs can be requested for HTVI-scope transactions.
- **Multiple adjustments:** The IRS can make several adjustments for a single HTVI transaction.
- **Open/closed year adjustments:** Possible to adjust in open years for amounts pertaining to closed years, including corresponding adjustments (generally via MAP request).

## Intra-group Services

Treas. Reg. section 1.482-9 provides rules consistent with TPG Chapter VII.

**Low value-adding services:** The services cost method in section 1.482-9(b) is consistent with the TPG simplified approach for low value-adding intra-group services.

## Financial Transactions

Treas. Reg. section 1.482-2 covers intercompany loans, consistent with TPG Chapter X. The US framework does not cover all financial transaction types addressed in Chapter X.

**Outside the TP framework:**
- IRC section 163(j): limits interest deductions (BEPS Action 4)
- IRC section 267A: limits deductions for interest and payments in hybrid situations (BEPS Action 2)

## Cost Contribution Arrangements

Treas. Reg. section 1.482-7 provides rules for cost sharing arrangements (CSAs), consistent with TPG Chapter VIII.

## Documentation and Penalties

**Documentation is voluntary** but provides critical penalty protection. The US does not require a Master File or Local File consistent with TPG Chapter V. Required filings:

- **Country-by-Country Report:** Required for US MNE groups with annual revenue of $850 million or more. (Treas. Reg. section 1.6038-4(h))
- **Form 5472:** Information returns for controlled transactions.
- **Penalty protection documentation:** Requirements set forth in Treas. Reg. section 1.6662-6(d).

**Penalties:** IRC section 6662 provides penalties for substantial valuation misstatements and gross valuation misstatements. Documentation meeting specified requirements may reduce or eliminate penalties. (IRC section 6662(e) and (h))

## Dispute Resolution

Available mechanisms:

- Enhanced engagement / cooperative compliance programmes
- **Advance Pricing Agreements (APAs):** unilateral, bilateral, and multilateral (Rev. Procs. 2015-40 and 2015-41)
- International Compliance Assurance Programme (ICAP)
- Mutual Agreement Procedures (MAP)

## Simplified and Streamlined Approach (Amount B)

The US adopted the SSA via Notice 2025-4 (released 18 December 2024). Key features:

- **Elective** for in-scope tested parties resident in the US (safe harbour, not a mandatory rule).
- **Effective:** Taxable years beginning on or after 1 January 2025.
- **OES upper bound:** Generally 30%, but can be lower (not less than 20%) per Notice 2025-4, section 3.02(2).
- **Respects outcomes:** The US respects SSA outcomes applied by both covered and non-covered jurisdictions.

## Safe Harbours

**Loan safe haven interest rate:** 100% to 130% of the Applicable Federal Rate for certain intercompany loans. (Treas. Reg. section 1.482-2(a)(2)(iii))

## Administrative Procedures

- **No downward self-help:** A controlled taxpayer cannot file an amended return to decrease taxable income. Only the IRS can make adjustments. (Treas. Reg. section 1.482-1(a)(3))
- **Year-end adjustments:** Allowed, subject to Treas. Reg. section 1.482-1(a)(3).
- **Secondary adjustments:** Allowed — may be treated as a dividend, capital contribution (Treas. Reg. section 1.482-1(g)(3)), or electively as a loan (Rev. Proc. 99-32).

## PE Profit Attribution

For 57 US treaties, profits are attributed based on the business profits article. Seven treaties (post-2010) apply the Authorised OECD Approach (AOA): Belgium, Bulgaria, Canada, Germany, Iceland, Japan, United Kingdom. The US does not apply the AOA to pre-2010 treaties. The US Model tax treaty follows the AOA as the preferred position for new treaties.
